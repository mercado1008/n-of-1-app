Diet and Lifestyle Considerations

The following considerations are surfaced for practitioner-led discussion. They sit alongside the recommended formulation rather than substituting for it.

Folate source

The practitioner may wish to discuss avoidance of synthetic folic acid (fortified cereals, breads, non-prescription multivitamins containing folic acid) and preference for whole-food folate and reduced folate supplements (calcium folinate, methylfolate) given the SLC19A1, MTHFR, and MTHFD1 variants documented above.

Rationale  Slow folate transport and reduced MTHFR/MTHFD1 flux favour reduced folate forms; unmetabolised folic acid may interfere with folate receptor activity.

Histamine and DAO support

The practitioner may wish to explore lower-histamine dietary patterns and identify any personal food triggers, given the slow DAO haplotype and slow MAOA.

Rationale  Reduced DAO activity elevates gut and systemic histamine burden; slow MAOA further reduces N-methylhistamine clearance.

Alcohol consumption

Practitioner discussion regarding minimising alcohol may be considered — particularly wine, champagne, and beer — given DAO, MAOA, and NAT2 slow status.

Rationale  Alcohol raises histamine load and acetaldehyde burden; reactive aldehydes rely on ALDH2/ADH1B (not genotyped here) for clearance.

Methyl-donor loading caution

Given the intermediate COMT haplotype combined with slow MAOA, the practitioner may wish to avoid supraphysiologic methyl-donor stacks (e.g. high-dose SAMe or TMG) and use pulse-dosing when methylation support is introduced.

Rationale  Slow COMT + slow MAOA increases catecholamine accumulation risk when methyl-donor drive is high.

Cruciferous vegetable intake

Regular intake of cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage, Brussels sprouts, kale) may be discussed with the patient — supporting Phase II detoxification, Nrf2, and oestrogen metabolism.

Rationale  Supports GSTA1, SOD2, CYP1B1 balance identified in the genotype.

Choline / betaine intake

Adequate dietary choline (eggs, liver, legumes) or supplemental choline/betaine may be discussed given MTHFD1 and BHMT slow variants, particularly relevant for women of reproductive age.

Rationale  MTHFD1 and BHMT slow variants increase choline demand; choline is generally underconsumed by women.

Iron and selenium status

The practitioner may wish to confirm iron and selenium status (ferritin, transferrin saturation, and where possible red-cell selenium) before extended supplementation with either mineral, given the GP-only submission.

Rationale  GPX4 activity depends on selenium sufficiency; several dopamine and serotonin synthesis enzymes require iron.

Environmental xenobiotic reduction

Practitioner discussion regarding reducing exposure to organophosphate pesticides, plastics/BPA, and smoke may be considered given the PON1, GSTA1, SOD2, and CYP1B1 profile.

Rationale  This genotype profile suggests reduced xenobiotic detoxification headroom.

Sleep and stress hygiene

Attention to stress management, consistent sleep timing, and daylight exposure may be discussed — supporting the TPH2/serotonin pathway and MAOA regulation.

Rationale  Vitamin D and daylight enhance brain TPH2; chronic stress modulates MAOA activity.
